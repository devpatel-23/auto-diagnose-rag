BRAKES, SUSPENSION AND STEERING REPAIR GUIDE
=============================================

SECTION 1: BRAKE SYSTEM
--------------------------
Brake Pad Replacement:
Symptoms of worn pads:
- Squealing (wear indicator touching rotor — replace soon)
- Grinding (metal on metal — replace immediately, rotor likely damaged)
- Pulsating pedal (warped rotors)
- Increased stopping distance
- Brake warning light (low pad sensor on some vehicles)

Pad thickness measurement:
- New pad: ~10-12mm
- Service limit: 2-3mm minimum
- Replace when: 3mm or less (do not wait for grinding)

Brake Rotor Inspection:
- Measure rotor thickness with micrometer.
- Minimum thickness is stamped on rotor edge (e.g., "MIN THICK 20MM").
- Check for deep grooves, heat cracks (spider-web cracks = replace immediately).
- Runout measurement: Max 0.002" (0.05mm) runout — above this causes pedal pulsation.
- Replace or machine (turn) rotors when replacing pads if worn or grooved.

Brake Caliper Issues:
- Sticking caliper: One wheel overheats, drags, vehicle pulls when braking.
- Seized piston: Can't compress piston to install new pads — replace caliper.
- Caliper rebuild: Replace rubber seals and boots if piston not severely corroded.

Brake Fluid Service:
- Brake fluid absorbs moisture over time (hygroscopic) — reduces boiling point.
- Change interval: Every 2 years or 30,000 miles regardless of appearance.
- Types: DOT 3, DOT 4, DOT 5.1 (glycol-based, mixable), DOT 5 (silicone, NOT mixable).
- Always use manufacturer-specified type. Never mix DOT 5 with others.
- Test: Brake fluid test strip checks moisture content.

Brake Bleeding Procedure:
- Bleed order: Start furthest from master cylinder (typically RR, LR, RF, LF).
- Two-person method: Helper pumps pedal, holds on "down," you open bleeder screw, close before pedal rises.
- Speed bleeder / vacuum bleeder: One-person method.
- Bleed until no air bubbles, fluid runs clear.

ABS System:
- ABS warning light: Scan for codes. Common causes: Wheel speed sensor failure, tone ring damage.
- Do NOT attempt to fix ABS without proper scan tool.
- ABS pump/module: Expensive, often requires programming.
- Vehicle still stops without ABS but loses anti-lock function.

SECTION 2: SUSPENSION SYSTEM
-------------------------------
Shock Absorbers and Struts:

Signs of worn shocks/struts:
- Bouncing excessively after bumps (more than 1-2 bounces)
- Nose dive under braking
- Body roll in corners
- Uneven/cupping tire wear
- Clunking over bumps
- Vehicle sits lower on one corner

Strut replacement:
- Always replace in pairs (both fronts or both rears).
- Quick strut assembly: Easiest — strut, spring, mount as one unit.
- Individual strut: Requires spring compressor to safely remove spring (dangerous without correct tool).
- Coilovers: Adjustable height/damping, used for performance or restoration lowering.
- After replacement: Alignment required.

Ball Joints:
- Worn ball joints are DANGEROUS — can separate causing complete loss of control.
- Test: Jack up vehicle, grasp tire at top and bottom, check for vertical play.
  Upper: More than 1/8" play = replace.
  Lower: Most have zero tolerance — any play = replace.
- Grease-able ball joints should be greased at every oil change.

Tie Rod Ends:
- Check: Grasp tie rod, try to move in/out — any play = replace.
- Symptoms: Wandering steering, uneven tire wear, clunking on turns.
- Always check alignment after replacement.

Wheel Bearings:
- Symptoms: Humming/growling noise that changes with speed, not with engine RPM.
  * Noise louder on left turns = right wheel bearing (load shifts to right bearing).
  * Noise louder on right turns = left wheel bearing.
- Test: Jack vehicle safely, spin wheel by hand — rough/grinding feeling = bad bearing.
- Modern integrated hub assemblies: Replace entire hub unit.
- Pressed-in bearings: Require press to remove/install.

Control Arm Bushings:
- Symptoms: Clunking over bumps, poor alignment retention, vehicle handling feels vague.
- Inspect for cracking, tearing, or complete failure of rubber.
- Polyurethane vs rubber: Poly is firmer and longer lasting but transmits more road noise.

SECTION 3: STEERING SYSTEM
-----------------------------
Power Steering (Hydraulic):

Low power steering fluid:
- Symptoms: Heavy steering, whining when turning wheel.
- Check reservoir — add correct fluid (PS fluid or specific type per manufacturer).
- Milky fluid = water contamination — flush system.

Power Steering Pump Failure:
- Symptoms: Loud whining/moaning, especially on full lock turns.
- Test: Pressure test with PS pressure gauge.
- Replacement: Prime new pump by filling reservoir and turning wheel lock-to-lock multiple times.

Power Steering Rack Leak:
- Signs: Fluid on ground under steering rack, boots wet with fluid.
- Minor leak: PS stop leak additives (temporary fix only).
- Proper fix: Rebuild or replace steering rack.

Electric Power Steering (EPS):
- No fluid to check.
- EPS warning light: Usually a fault code — scan with OBD reader.
- Common faults: Torque sensor, EPS control module, motor failure.
- Do NOT attempt to overpower or manually steer if EPS fails — get power steering service.

Steering Alignment:
Types:
- Toe: Most critical — incorrect toe destroys tires rapidly (feathering wear pattern).
- Camber: Tilt of wheel — important for tire wear and handling.
- Caster: Forward/backward tilt of steering axis — affects straight-line stability.

When alignment is needed:
- After any suspension/steering component replacement.
- After hitting a large pothole or curb.
- If vehicle pulls to one side.
- If steering wheel is off-center.
- Annually on older vehicles.

Tire Rotation and Balance:
- Rotation interval: Every 5,000-7,500 miles.
- Patterns: Front-to-rear (directional), cross-rotate (non-directional).
- Balance: Rebalance when vibration felt in steering wheel (typically front tires) or seat (rear tires).
- Vibration at specific MPH that goes away = unbalanced tire/wheel.
