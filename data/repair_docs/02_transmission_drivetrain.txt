TRANSMISSION AND DRIVETRAIN REPAIR GUIDE
=========================================

SECTION 1: AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION
------------------------------------
Common Symptoms and Causes:

Slipping Gears (RPM rises but car doesn't accelerate proportionally):
- Low or burnt transmission fluid — check dipstick, fluid should be red/pink and not smell burnt.
- Worn clutch packs inside transmission.
- Failing transmission solenoids.
- Fix: First drain and refill transmission fluid with OEM-spec fluid. If slipping continues, transmission service or rebuild needed.

Delayed Engagement (long pause before drive/reverse engages):
- Low fluid, cold fluid in winter, or worn valve body.
- Let engine warm up 30-60 seconds before driving in cold weather.
- Check fluid level and condition.

Harsh or Rough Shifting:
- Wrong fluid type used.
- Worn throttle position sensor causing incorrect shift points.
- Faulty shift solenoids.
- Check transmission fluid level and type first.

Transmission Fluid Service:
- Check interval: Every 30,000-60,000 miles (not "lifetime" regardless of what manual says).
- Fluid color guide:
  * Bright red/pink = Good
  * Dark red = Getting old, service soon
  * Brown/black = Urgent service needed
  * Milky/foamy = Water contamination (serious — check for cooler line leak)
- Always use manufacturer-specified fluid (Dexron, Mercon, ATF+4 etc — these are NOT interchangeable).

SECTION 2: MANUAL TRANSMISSION
---------------------------------
Clutch System:

Clutch Slipping (high RPM, poor acceleration, burning smell):
- Worn clutch disc (friction material worn to rivets).
- Oil contamination on clutch (rear main seal or transmission input shaft seal leak).
- Clutch replacement: Full kit — disc, pressure plate, throw-out bearing, pilot bearing.
- Clutch life: Typically 80,000-150,000 miles depending on driving style.

Clutch Pedal Issues:
- Soft/spongy pedal (hydraulic clutch): Air in hydraulic line — bleed clutch slave cylinder.
- High pedal engagement: Adjust clutch pedal free play (cable system) or check hydraulic components.
- Clutch pedal sticks to floor: Clutch slave cylinder failure.

Gear Grinding:
- Synchronizer rings worn (inside transmission).
- Low gear oil — check manual transmission fluid level.
- Synchro problem: Usually isolated to one gear (e.g., 2nd gear grind = 2nd gear synchro).

Manual Transmission Fluid:
- Most use 75W-90 GL-4 gear oil (check model specific — some use engine oil or ATF).
- Change interval: Every 30,000-50,000 miles.

SECTION 3: DIFFERENTIAL AND AXLES
------------------------------------
Differential Noise (whining, howling on deceleration/acceleration):
- Low gear oil in differential.
- Worn ring and pinion gears.
- Worn carrier bearings.
- Check/change differential fluid first (75W-90 or 75W-140 for limited-slip).

Limited Slip Differential (LSD) Chatter:
- Symptom: Chattering/shuddering in tight turns.
- Cause: Wrong fluid (must use LSD-specific additive or LSD-rated fluid).
- Fix: Drain and refill with correct LSD fluid. Do not use standard gear oil in LSD without additive.

CV Axle (Front-Wheel Drive and AWD):
- Clicking noise on turns = outer CV joint failure (torn CV boot, grease contaminated).
- Clunking on acceleration = inner CV joint failure.
- Inspection: Check rubber boots — if cracked or torn, replace before joint fails.
- Replacement: Remanufactured axle shaft assembly (cost-effective), or rebuild with new CV joints and boots.

SECTION 4: FOUR-WHEEL DRIVE / AWD SYSTEMS
-------------------------------------------
Transfer Case Service:
- Check fluid: Should be changed every 30,000-50,000 miles.
- Symptoms of low/dirty fluid: Whining, hesitation to engage 4WD.
- Common fluids: ATF (some), specific transfer case fluid (others) — check manual.

4WD Engagement Issues:
- 4WD won't engage: Check shift linkage, vacuum lines (on older systems), or electronic actuator.
- Grinding when engaging: Do NOT engage 4WD on dry pavement — use only on slippery or loose surfaces.
- 4WD stuck engaged: Actuator fault, binding in transfer case.

AWD System Binding:
- Binding/hopping in turns with AWD: Tire size mismatch across axles (even temporary spare causes this).
- Ensure all 4 tires are same brand/model/size and worn evenly.

SECTION 5: DRIVESHAFT AND U-JOINTS
-------------------------------------
U-Joint Failure Symptoms:
- Clunking/vibration when accelerating or decelerating (especially from stop).
- Vibration at specific speed that gets worse.
- Visible rust, play, or dried grease on U-joint caps.

U-Joint Replacement:
- Mark driveshaft orientation before removal (balance marks).
- Use proper U-joint press tool to remove bearing caps.
- Pack new U-joint with grease before installation.
- Check center bearing (carrier bearing) on two-piece driveshafts.

Driveshaft Balance:
- Vibration that increases with speed = balance issue (after U-joint work, accident, or bent shaft).
- Requires driveshaft balancing machine at driveline shop.
