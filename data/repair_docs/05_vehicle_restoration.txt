CLASSIC VEHICLE RESTORATION GUIDE
====================================

SECTION 1: RESTORATION PLANNING AND ASSESSMENT
-------------------------------------------------
Project Assessment - The Three Questions:
1. What is the vehicle's current condition? (Rust, mechanical, interior)
2. What is the end goal? (Driver, show car, restomod, concours)
3. What is the budget and timeline?

Rust Assessment (Most Critical Factor):
Rust Categories:
- Surface rust: Just the paint/surface — easiest to fix.
- Scale rust: Bubbling, texture under surface — requires grinding and treatment.
- Penetrating rust: Holes, structural rust — requires metal fabrication.

Where to look:
- Floor pans (lift carpet, check from underneath)
- Frame rails and crossmembers (use light and mirror)
- Rocker panels (between front and rear doors, often hidden by trim)
- Wheel arches (upper inner and outer)
- Trunk floor and spare tire well
- Lower firewall
- Door bottoms and window seals
- Battery tray area

Rust Severity Guide:
- Surface/light scale: DIY repair possible — grind, treat, fill, paint.
- Moderate panel rust: Some panel fabrication or replacement needed.
- Severe structural rust: Frame damage — requires professional metal fabrication or frame-off restoration.
- Beyond economical repair: More rust than solid metal — walk away unless the vehicle has exceptional value.

Budget Planning for Restoration:
Basic driver restoration:
- Engine rebuild: $1,500-$4,000
- Transmission service/rebuild: $500-$2,500
- Brake system: $500-$1,500
- Suspension rebuild: $1,000-$3,000
- Basic bodywork and paint: $5,000-$15,000
- Interior: $1,000-$5,000
- Total rough estimate: $10,000-$30,000+

Full show-quality restoration: $30,000-$100,000+

SECTION 2: ENGINE RESTORATION
--------------------------------
Engine Assessment:
- Compression test: All cylinders should be within 10% of each other. Below 100 PSI = rebuild.
- Leak-down test: More precise — pressurize cylinder, measure leakage. Over 20% = worn rings/valves.
- Oil pressure: Cold start should show 40-60 PSI, idle warm minimum 10 PSI per 1000 RPM.
- Check for: Cracks in block/head, worn journals (measure with micrometer), cam lobe wear.

Engine Rebuild Basics:
Short block rebuild (bottom end):
1. Disassemble and clean all parts (hot tank or solvent cleaning).
2. Measure all bores, journals, tolerances.
3. Machine work: Bore cylinders, hone, grind crankshaft journals if needed.
4. New parts: Piston rings, rod and main bearings, gasket set.
5. Assembly: Use plastigage to verify bearing clearances, torque to spec in sequence.

Cylinder head work:
- Valve job: Grind valve seats and faces for proper seal.
- Valve guides: Check for wear, replace or knurl if worn.
- Head surface: Machine flat if warped (check with straightedge).
- Springs: Check spring pressure, replace if at or below spec.

Popular Classic Engine Rebuilds:
- Small Block Chevy (283/327/350): Most parts available, many rebuild kits, extremely common.
- Ford Windsor (289/302/351W): Well-supported, many aftermarket options.
- Mopar 318/360: Durable, less glamorous but very reliable.
- Pontiac 400/455: Torque monsters, parts harder to find.

SECTION 3: BODY AND PAINT RESTORATION
---------------------------------------
Body Work Process:
1. Disassembly: Remove all chrome, trim, glass, rubber.
2. Strip paint: Media blast (gentlest), chemical strip, or sand.
3. Rust repair: Cut out rust, weld in patch panels or new metal.
4. Body work: Straighten panels, lead or filler for imperfections.
5. Primer: Epoxy primer (best corrosion protection), then filler primer.
6. Block sanding: Achieve flat, straight panels.
7. Sealer/base coat/clear coat: Professional paint application.

Body Panel Straightening:
- Dent repair tools: Body hammers, dollies, body spoons.
- Pulling: Stud welder puller, frame straightening equipment.
- Filler (Bondo): Used for small imperfections only — not for large areas or structural repair.
- Lead filling: Traditional method, superior but requires skill and safety precautions (lead dust is toxic).

Welding for Body Work:
- MIG welding: Most common for patch panels, floor pans.
- Spot welding: Factory method for panel joining.
- Tig welding: Cleanest welds, good for thin metal and visible areas.
- Metal thickness: Body panels are typically 18-20 gauge — control heat to prevent warping.

Paint Systems:
- Single stage: Color only, no clear coat (original for most pre-1980s cars), easier to spot repair.
- Base coat/clear coat: Modern standard, deeper shine, harder to spot repair.
- Urethane: Durable, most commonly used today.
- Lacquer: Traditional, beautiful depth, original for classics but not durable (chips, cracks over time).
- Color matching: Provide paint code (door jamb sticker on most vehicles) for proper match.

Rust Prevention After Repair:
- Internal cavities: Cavity wax or rust-inhibiting oil in door cavities, frame rails, rocker panels.
- Undercoating: Rubberized undercoating on floor pans and undercarriage.
- Epoxy primer: Best foundation — excellent moisture barrier.
- POR-15: Excellent for treating existing rust and painting over it as a sealer.

SECTION 4: INTERIOR RESTORATION
----------------------------------
Upholstery:
- Seat foam: Replace if compressed or crumbling. Universal foam can be cut to shape.
- Vinyl restoration: Clean with vinyl cleaner, condition, professional dye if faded.
- Leather: Clean and condition regularly. Professional re-dye or replacement for severe cracks.
- Carpet: Cut and bound carpet kits available for most classics. Jute underlayment for sound deadening.
- Headliner: Sagging headliner — re-glue with headliner adhesive or replace board and fabric.

Dashboard Restoration:
- Cracked dashpads: Repair with dashboard repair kit, cover with new vinyl, or source replacement.
- Gauge restoration: Clean faces, restore needles, new backlighting (LED conversion).
- Chrome trim: Polish, re-chrome, or replace with reproduction pieces.

Sound Deadening:
- Products: Dynamat, Second Skin, Fatmat — butyl rubber mats applied to floor, doors, firewall.
- Reduces road noise, improves stereo performance.
- Apply to: Floor pan, inside doors, trunk, firewall.

SECTION 5: RESTOMOD GUIDE
---------------------------
Restomod Philosophy: Classic looks, modern performance and reliability.

Popular Upgrades:
Powertrain:
- Engine swaps: LS engine swap (modern Chevy V8) very popular — fits many engine bays.
- Fuel injection conversion: TBI or EFI kits for carbureted engines (better cold start, fuel economy, reliability).
- Overdrive transmission: Add overdrive for highway cruising (700R4, 4L60E, Tremec 5/6-speed).

Suspension:
- Subframe connectors: Critical for unibody cars — eliminates body flex.
- Coilover conversion: Modern adjustable coilovers for classic applications.
- Rack and pinion steering: Converts from recirculating ball for sharper response.
- Rear end upgrade: Change to modern rear axle with better gear ratios and disc brakes.

Brakes:
- Disc brake conversion: Front and/or rear disc from drums — major safety improvement.
- Brake booster: Power brake conversion for better pedal feel.
- Wilwood/Baer big brake kits: For serious performance stopping.

Modern Electronics:
- Vintage Air or Classic Auto Air: Modern A/C in period-correct housing.
- Dakota Digital gauges: Classic look, modern sensors and accuracy.
- Bluetooth stereo: Hidden behind original radio face.
- LED lighting: Interior, taillights, engine bay — modern reliability.
